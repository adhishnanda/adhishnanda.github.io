Cost-Aware, Governance-First RAG for AI Regulation

Supervised by Prof. Dr. Iftikhar Ahmed and Prof. Dr. Talha Ali Khan.

A retrieval and answering system for compliance questions across four regulatory corpora, with adaptive model routing so generation cost is controlled without losing answer quality.

The problem

Compliance teams ask precise questions about the EU AI Act, GDPR, NIST AI RMF and ISO/IEC 42001. A plain RAG system either answers imprecisely or sends every query to the most expensive model. The thesis asked whether a governance-focused retrieval design and adaptive routing could hold quality while cutting cost.

Retrieval

Symbolic pre-retrieval catches article and clause references exactly. Weighted sparse-dense fusion handles paraphrased questions. A cross-encoder reranks the fused candidates. Context precision on the 25-query annotated gold set: 0.97.

Cost routing

A rule-based router assigns each query to a cost tier. Compared with always routing to the expensive tier, generation cost fell 72.2% while generation quality, scored by LLM-as-judge and RAGAS, reached 109.4% of the baseline.

Evaluation

Three layers: LLM-as-judge scoring, RAGAS faithfulness and context precision, and a post-hoc counterfactual analysis comparing router decisions against an oracle. Results are shown in a Streamlit dashboard with a cost-quality Pareto view and per-query inspection.

Limitations

Models were served locally on constrained hardware. Cost is measured as relative generation cost across tiers rather than billed spend. The gold set is 25 queries.
